## E. Top findings

1. Pressure and load cannot be separated cleanly. Technical sources support coupled pressure, deflection, footprint, cyclic strain, hysteresis and temperature behavior. Absolute aircraft/truck values remain non-transferable to historical racing tires without a bridge.
2. Surface/slip heating and bulk/carcass heating are distinguishable inputs. NASA work supports retaining separate cyclic-deflection and slip/braking heat paths with measurement-based validation.
3. Wear is condition dependent. Controlled NASA tire work supports wear sensitivity to distance, slip, surface and temperature, but does not supply a historical racing wear-to-grip curve.
4. Vehicle/load-dependent compound applicability is now corroborated by period race examples and modern Dunlop vintage-racing guidance. It remains an architecture candidate, not a hard weight threshold.
5. A period 1966 survey distinguishes radial and cross-ply nylon racing products within the same supplier era. Brand alone is insufficient to assign construction.
6. The reviewed 1959 Reims report gives useful high-heat durability context, but not a universal stint-life number.
7. Dunlop's retrospective says `RS5`; this staging record does not silently relabel it `R5`.
8. Modern reproduction-tire pressure and dimension tables are valuable validation/context sources but are explicitly separated from original-period racing evidence.
9. GT40 evidence now supports Goodyear/Firestone event and failure context, including wet-tire switching and high-speed tread trouble, but exact pressures, loaded radius and stiffness remain open.
10. The 1993-1996 GT record is stronger for chronology, sanction context, regulations and supplier/event routing than for pressure, temperature, radial stiffness, wear or heat-cycle behavior.
